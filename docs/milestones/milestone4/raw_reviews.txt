Names of your group members: Angela Watson, Cameron Hensley, Sripriya

Review for group - 6


Title of the project being reviewed: Physics-Informed Machine Learning for Regional
Geomagnetic Hazard Forecasting


Summary of their solution:
Outline your understanding of what problem they are solving and their
approach/methodology.


The project builds per-station binary classifiers to predict dangerous geomagnetic
disturbances one hour ahead at four high-latitude monitoring sites (ABK, MEA, SOD,
YKC), using ten years of multi-layer minute-resolution data (268 engineered features),
comparing baseline and ML models (including LightGBM and LSTM), and evaluating
mainly with the Heidke Skill Score to provide station-level hazard warnings where
current forecasts are only global.


Three strengths of their approach:
​      What are the positives?
    1.​ S1: Rigorous leakage prevention. They enforce strict temporal validity so models
       only see information available at prediction time (at least one hour before the
       event), using multiple programmatic checks: backward-only rolling windows,
       time-ordered splits, no future-looking columns, and automated audits. That
       reduces a common failure mode in time-series forecasting.
    2.​ S2: Sun-to-ground data with physics-informed features. The dataset spans five physical
       layers (Sun X-ray, solar wind, high/low orbit magnetics, ground stations) over about ten years
       at one-minute resolution, giving earlier upstream signals than ground-only work. They
       engineer 268 features, including domain quantities (e.g., Newell coupling, southward Bz) that
       reflect how energy couples to the ground, which should help performance and
       generalization.
    3.​ S3: Design and evaluation fit for rare, station-specific hazards. They train
       separate binary classifiers per station (ABK, MEA, SOD, YKC) instead of one
       global model, respecting different event rates and local responses. For rare
       positive events, they prioritize Heidke Skill Score over accuracy and tune
       decision thresholds on validation data, aligning metrics with operational use.


Three weaknesses of their approach:
​      What can be done better and how in your opinion can they improve?
    1.​ W1: Limited execution, no end-to-end or model results yet. Work is
       demonstrated for only one month (January 2015); the full 2015–2024 pipeline
       has not been run. No models are trained or evaluated, including
       persistence/climatology baselines, so it is unclear whether the data design and
       feature engineering improve prediction. Metrics like HSS and PR-AUC have no
       reference point until baselines exist.
    2.​ W2: Rare-event and station-level strategy are underdeveloped. EDA shows the
       target is extremely rare, but the plan does not clearly address class imbalance
       at training time (threshold tuning and HSS help at evaluation but are not a
       substitute for training-time handling, especially for LogReg and LightGBM).
       Separate models per station need stronger justification: some sites may have
       too few positives for reliable models, and tradeoffs (compute, tuning, which
       station to prioritize) are not spelled out, including a fallback if all four cannot be
       finished.
    3.​ W3: Scope ambition and data-quality risks. Planning five model types (including
       LSTM as a stretch goal) plus four station-specific models may be too much for
       the timeline; effort might be better focused on a well-evaluated LightGBM (and
       baselines) for at least one station. OMNI solar wind — critical for features like Bz
       and speed — has ~23% missingness from satellite gaps; missingness flags
       mitigate but leave a large share of primary solar-wind measurements absent.


Other comments:
​      Anything else you wish to add.
Review was slides-only (no video), so motivation and design are clearer than the
team’s reasoning and live walkthrough. The deck is strong on literature-to-design
mapping (more data sources, station-level prediction, leakage controls), reproducibility
(fast demo commands and artifact pipelines), and tuning plans (e.g., scale_pos_weight
for LightGBM). Still, most implementation is ahead of them—full pipeline, training, and
evaluation are not done—and without a recording it is harder to judge whether they
can finish in time or how they will prioritize when scope is tight.
